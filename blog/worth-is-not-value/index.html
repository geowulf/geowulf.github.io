Worth Is Not Value

If you lost your job tomorrow, would you be worth less on Thursday? Worth is given. Value is assigned.

By Greg Woolf · November 9, 2026

If you lost your job tomorrow, would you be worth less on Thursday?

That's a question people keep bringing up in my car. Not in those exact words — in different words, same ache. Somebody's hours got cut. Somebody got passed over. Somebody's body quit cooperating and now they can't do the thing they built their whole identity on. And underneath all of it is the same question: if I'm not producing, do I still matter?

Here's the thing. I think we've got the words mixed up. Worth and value — we use them like they're the same word, and they're not. Not even close.

Worth is given. Value is assigned.

Worth is the part that's set. You were born with it. It's the divine part, the part nobody handed you and nobody can take. It's not a grade. It's not a score. It's just... set. You matter because you're here. Full stop.

Value is the importance we put on things. And that's fine — value is a good tool, for things. A price tag. A priority list. A trade-off. The trouble starts when we point that tool at a person. When we start grading a soul the way we'd grade a product. When a human being gets a price tag.

The feeling is real. The measurement is wrong.

And look, I get why it feels real. If your worth can't be destroyed, why does it feel destroyed? I've sat with that feeling too — shame, failure, comparison, the month the money ran thin. Feeling worthless is real. I'm not going to sit here and tell you the feeling isn't real. The feeling is real. The measurement is wrong.

Because here's the test I keep coming back to. If everything you produce falls away — the job, the title, the paycheck, the thing you're good at — does your worth change? If it doesn't, then you were measuring the wrong thing all along. You were weighing the gift on a scale built for groceries.

Who taught you that?

And here's the question underneath that one, the one I always come back to: who taught you that? Somebody taught you what makes you matter. Maybe it was your parents. Maybe school. Maybe a boss. Maybe the feed, every single day, telling you you're only as good as your output. That belief has a biography. It was taught to you. And anything that was taught can be examined.

So no — value isn't the villain here. Value has its place. It belongs on things, on choices, on priorities. It just doesn't belong on people. You don't put a price tag on a soul.

Worth is given. Value is assigned. You can't earn what's given, and you can't lose it by failing at what's assigned.

So — what were you taught your worth was?
